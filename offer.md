# The Offer — Grand Slam Offer (Hormozi method)

Built with the exact anatomy taught in the vault's BC7 Day 5 notes (`hormozi-method.md`): **audience → problem → solution → bonuses → guarantee**, then scarcity, urgency, and the value stack. Doctrine held throughout: *"an offer should feel scary to the seller."*

## 1. Audience / ICP

Founders with a working business ($1M+ revenue or equivalent traction) who are still the operator: chief decision-maker, approver-of-everything, on every thread. Success happened; it happened *to them*. They have or can afford help — they just don't have a system for handing work over, or the nerve. Secondary identity: they suspect AI is about to compress their niche and they're tired of pretending that's fine.

## 2. The problem (in their words)

"I built this thing, and now I *am* this thing. If I stop, it stops."

Vault framing: "You Are The Bottleneck" (Seven Laws, Law 1). The Control Paradox — overworking/refusing to let go hurts growth. And the timer the founder can feel but can't see: AI compresses the innovation lifecycle, so "the strategy that built your business is now killing it"; the competitor running five parallel bets while you optimize your one thing.

## 3. The solution (the vehicle)

**Star Founder — a 10-week live cohort with Kasim Aslam.** Twenty hours live. You hand off your ten most draining jobs using Kasim's actual system — Miracle Briefs, Delegate Messy, guardrails not checklists, the 2-6-2 standard, celebrate-first-correct-separately — and install the weekly cadence that keeps the machine evolving without you (mutation meetings, the optionality map, Core + Edge).

Proof it works on humans other than Kasim: his third EA — a Ukrainian student hired at $1,200/mo — ran his SoftBank-backed exit from a war zone and is now his 50/50 partner. One Pareto EA took a single "just decide" and built a septic business to ~$14M/yr revenue and $1.5M in government contracts; Kasim has never worked a day in it. A 2.5-minute voice note to Ivan became a self-storage facility bought at $490k against ~$750k retail — a quarter-million in profit.

## 5. The guarantee (conditional, Hormozi type 3)

**The Bottleneck Guarantee.** Finish the ten weeks. Hand off your ten jobs with real Miracle Briefs. If your business doesn't run measurably better without you — show us the briefs, and every dollar comes back. No forms, no fight.

Conditional on purpose: the method only fails when the founder won't let go, and the guarantee is engineered to select for people who will. (Doctrine echo: Pareto Talent ships a lifetime replacement guarantee on every EA — this brand guarantees outcomes, not feelings.)

## 6. Scarcity & urgency (real, not manufactured)

- **One room.** Kasim runs one cohort at a time, alongside his portfolio. Seats capped so every founder's actual business gets torn down live. (First Pareto cohort ever had 11 people; the pattern is small rooms.)
- **Hard start date.** The next cohort starts **January 11, 2027** *(placeholder — swap for the real date)*. Applications close when seats fill or on the cut-off, whichever comes first.
- Doctrine from the vault: "A live event outsells a course because of true urgency. Without them people defer forever."

## 7. The value equation, run

**Value = (Dream outcome × Likelihood) ÷ (Time delay × Effort & sacrifice)**

- **Dream outcome ↑** — not "tips," a business that passes the 90-day test: *"You own a business when you can leave for 90 days and when you come back things are more efficient and more profitable."*
- **Likelihood ↑** — the operator-to-owner arc is the one Kasim has run six times (three exits), taught to thousands of founders, and priced at $2,000/hr.
- **Time delay ↓** — first delegation goes out in week one. Result promised in weeks, not years.
- **Effort & sacrifice ↓** — the system is built for tired operators: briefs, not SOPs; guardrails, not checklists; "export chaos, import order."
